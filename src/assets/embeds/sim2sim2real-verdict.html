The sim2sim2real Verdict

Does a frozen model of the robot's own kinematics, learned in a clean simulator, carry a behaviour into the randomized one? We built the mechanism, ran it against our honest control, and it did not.

Paper: arXiv 2606.02636v2Control: sk_lag_r128_s07 training runs, 4 plants, 2 laddersAll numbers single seed, n = 1000 episodes

The imitation-on-kinematics mechanism adds nothing measurable on this robot. With a warm start it ties the plain warm start; from scratch it lands below the plain control at the 3000-iteration checkpoint; and with a reference learned on a plant outside the robot's neighbourhood it fails outright.

What did hold is the two-plant structure. A policy pretrained on a clean plant and dropped into the full domain-randomized one reaches the control's plateau in about a hundred iterations instead of seven hundred, and matches it at the full budget.

Everything here is one seed on one robot in one simulator. The tie is the honest reading; a small effect in either direction is still inside the noise.

1. What the paper proposes

Too Much of a Good Thing: When sim2real Efforts Impede Policy Learning is a position paper. Its argument: every domain-randomization axis added to make a policy robust also makes the learning problem harder, until the randomization itself is what stops the behaviour from being found. The proposed cure is to separate the two jobs.

Support phase
Idealized simulator
Learn the behaviour where nothing fights it. Alongside the policy, train a forward model that predicts the next kinematic state from the actor's own observation.
Transfer phase
High-fidelity simulator
Freeze the model. Retrain on the honest, randomized plant with a state-based imitation reward: stay close to what the idealized behaviour would have done next.
Deployment
Hardware
The policy keeps its interface; the forward model is discarded. Robustness came from the transfer plant, the behaviour from the support plant.

The paper gives the mechanism and one humanoid case study. It gives no reward formula, no architecture, no ablations, and no numbers. Everything below the mechanism is therefore our adaptation, and the verdict is on that adaptation.

2. Why it mattered here

Our single-phase pipeline trains PPO from scratch on the honest plant: a held action delay of 5 to 45 ms per episode, a log-uniform Coulomb friction decade, mass and gain multipliers, sensor noise and bias, pushes, asymmetry, and a curriculum. Every bench session that showed a gap became a new or wider axis. The list had stopped being a robustness tool and become system identification in disguise, with ranges that were guesses. The paper's promise was to learn the behaviour once and only rerun the cheap phase when the plant belief changed.

The control is sk_lag_r128_s0: 1024 environments, 128-step rollouts, 5000 iterations, 24 minibatches. On the honest plant it survives 0.870 of 1000 episodes at the last checkpoint and 0.886 at the era checkpoint 4500; on the clean plant, 0.996. Those are the numbers every arm below is measured against, with the same evaluator, seed and episode count.

3. What we built

A self-contained package that imports the existing environment and recipe without changing them. The actor keeps its 40-wide observation and voltage actions; the forward model rides in the checkpoint as extra top-level keys and both stock loaders accept the files unchanged.

5. Diagnosis: two faults, not one

Before changing anything we scored the frozen model directly. The scoring script rolls a policy out on a plant, applies the imitation kernel at every step exactly as the reward manager would, and splits the per-step reward by what the episode was doing: surviving to the cap, or the last half second before a fall.

Fault one, the plant. The repository's own log had the firmware chain measured at 11 to 13 ms, policies that do not cover a delay falling at it, and the real plant carrying about 20 ms-equivalent of phase. Delay is essential dynamics on this robot, not a nuisance to idealize away.

Fault two, the kernel. A controller that survives 89% of honest episodes sat 7.5 σ off the model's predictions and earned 0.04 of a possible 1. The width had been set by the frictionless plant's own deltas. A temperature τ = mse / ln 2, chosen so that a known-good controller earns about half credit while balancing, made the term attainable and gave it a clear margin between balancing and falling.

A note on reading logs: rewards in this environment are not scaled by the time step, and the logged episode reward is the episode sum divided by the 10 s cap. Our first reading of ladder-1's imitation scalar as "near saturation" was wrong; it was near zero. The diagnosis above is what the direct measurement showed.

7. The arm that isolates the term

B1 and B0 differ only in the imitation term and came out the same, which says the term adds nothing on top of a warm start. B1S asks whether it does anything on its own: the honest plant, the calibrated kernel, and a random initialization. It ran to iteration 3000 and was stopped there on request, so its comparison is the control's 3000 checkpoint: 0.832 against 0.877 on the honest plant, four times the run-to-run spread, with the same clean survival, a higher velocity error, and a weaker longest-delay bin at 0.661 against the control's 0.789 at the era checkpoint. Its training curve had crossed the control's repeatedly and averaged eight steps below it over the last thousand iterations. Read plainly: from a random initialization the term does not accelerate learning and, at this checkpoint, the policy it shapes is somewhat worse than the one shaped by the task reward alone. Whether that deficit would have closed by 5000 is unknown; the control itself moved by less than 0.01 between 3000 and 4999.

What held

  • The behaviour transfers through the actor's weights. A policy pretrained on the clean plant reaches the control's plateau in about a hundred honest-plant iterations instead of seven hundred, and ends at the same survival.
  • Both warm-started arms keep station better. Lateral drift halves and position error falls from 0.032 to 0.027 m in both, and B1's final yaw error is the lowest of the era. This is consistent across the two arms but it is still one seed each.
  • The diagnostic tooling. Scoring a frozen model against a known-good controller, before training on it, caught both faults in minutes.

What did not

  • The forward model as a transfer mechanism. With the plant inside the robot's neighbourhood, the warm start alone does everything the imitation arm does. With the plant outside it, no kernel width helps. From scratch, the term neither speeds learning nor changes the endpoint beyond noise.
  • The premise that randomization impedes learning here. PPO from scratch finds balancing under the full randomization in about seven hundred iterations. There is no exploration bottleneck for a reference to unlock. The paper's case is a humanoid, where there may be one.
  • "Idealized" as a free choice. The support plant has to keep the dynamics that define the task. For this robot that is friction and a real action delay, which we only knew from the hardware log.

Caveats we owe the reader

  • One seed per arm. Survival differences under about 0.02 are noise at this size. Nothing here reaches that bar in either direction.
  • Sim only. The hardware comparison against the deployed checkpoint was specified but not run.
  • Our adaptation, not the paper's. The horizon, the kernel, the weight and the observation-only model are our choices; a different one could behave differently, though the warm-start result bounds how much room there is.
  • The kernel temperature was calibrated on the control's rollouts. That is a small leakage of the control into the arm being compared with it; it made the term attainable, and the term still did nothing.
  • The clean plant already needed the 12 ms delay, which came from the hardware log. The method did not tell us which dynamics were essential; the bench did.
  • Cost accounting. The first-time cost of the two-phase route, 2000 clean iterations plus a short honest phase, is not lower than training from scratch once. The saving appears only on the second and later reruns.

9. What survives, and what to do with it

Keep: the clean-plant pretrain and the honest-plant warm start, without the imitation term. Keep the scoring script as a diagnostic.

Drop: the forward model from the training recipe.

Test next: a 500-iteration honest phase from the clean checkpoint on every gate, including the wobble gate and cross-engine play. If it matches the control, the domain-randomization question becomes a series of fifteen-minute reruns instead of five-hour ones, which is the problem the paper was brought in to solve. Then a second seed for the drift numbers, and the bench comparison.

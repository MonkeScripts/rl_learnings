The Split Loop

How a 100 Hz policy and a 500 Hz plant share one env.step() in mjlab. We asked whether the shared-pipeline layer is over-engineered, looked at how other projects structure the same loop, and decided to keep it.

branch lean_legsmjlab ManagerBasedRlEnvdecimation = 500 // 100 = 5

Decision: keep the shared plant layer as it is

  • The pipelines stay in wlbot/. They have four real callers: the mjlab trainer, PyBullet through SingleRobot, Newton, and the firmware-parity tests. One definition keeps a cross-engine difference meaning "the physics differs", never "a copy drifted".
  • The awkward mjlab wiring stays too, for now. It works. Its cost is readability, not correctness.
  • Escape hatch, if the wiring ever causes a real bug: subclass ManagerBasedRlEnv and override step() so its substep loop reads like SingleRobot.step (Isaac Lab's "Direct" workflow, done by hand).
  • Cleanups are batched for later, in one pass (listed at the end).

The question we started with

We know the plant is shared: ActuationPipeline, SensePipeline, DomainRandomizer and SingleRobot. But mjlab feels self-contained. We build an env config, hand it to ManagerBasedRlEnv, and write our own manager terms for mjlab to call. So what do our pipelines actually do, how do they talk to mjlab, and how do the two rates fit together?

The first mental model was a set of running components passing streams of data: policy → actuation pipeline → adapter → torque → mjlab → observations → adapter → sense pipeline → policy. That turned out to be the wrong way to see it.

What we learned

What is SingleRobot for?

It isn't part of the plant. It is the episode loop, written by hand for engines that don't bring a loop of their own. It owns the three plant objects plus a backend, and its step() is the whole simulation in about ten lines. It runs at num_envs=1, float64, on the CPU. The pipelines themselves are device-agnostic, and SingleRobot is what pins them there.

Callers: the PyBullet env WlbotBalanceEnv (evaluation only; the gymnasium dependency was dropped in c343e30, only its reset/step shape remains), crossplay.py, and tests. mjlab training never touches it.

self.pipeline.mix(action)                      # 100 Hz: store two volts
for _ in range(self.substeps):                 # 5 × 2 ms
    tau, h = self.pipeline.tick(omega)         # volts → torque
    true   = self.backend.substep(tau, ...)    # physics

obs = self._observe()                          # 100 Hz: read the obs delay ring

Are the pipelines "running" and streaming data to mjlab?

No. There is one Python thread. The pipelines are objects holding state: a delay ring, filter states, battery charge. They do something only when a method is called on them. Everything is driven by one function, env.step(action), which rsl_rl calls once per policy step. This is mjlab's own step (manager_based_rl_env.py), trimmed, with our code marked:

def step(self, action):                          # called at 100 Hz by rsl_rl
    self.action_manager.process_action(action)   # → WheelVoltageAction → pipeline.mix()
    for _ in range(self.cfg.decimation):         # 5
        self.action_manager.apply_action()
        self.scene.write_data_to_sim()           # → adapter.compute() → pipeline.tick(ω)
        self.sim.step()                          # MuJoCo integrates 2 ms
        self.scene.update(dt)                    # → adapter.update() → sense.tick(truth)
    self.termination_manager.compute()           # back at 100 Hz
    self.reward_manager.compute()                # → PPO, not the policy
    ...reset finished envs (DR event draws new plants)...
    self.obs_buf = self.observation_manager.compute()   # → StationKeepObs reads the obs ring

The "stream" feeling comes from state that survives between calls. "Rate" means how often something is called: each sim.step() advances simulated time by 2 ms, however fast the GPU actually runs.

How do 100 Hz and 500 Hz fit together?

The for _ in range(decimation) loop is the whole answer. It works in two directions, one expanding and one shrinking:

One policy step. Actuation expands: one command becomes five torques. Sensing shrinks: five filtered frames go in, and one delayed frame comes out.

Actuation expands with a hold

The same volts stay in pipeline.command for all five ticks, but the torque differs on each one. Back-EMF uses the wheel speed MuJoCo produced 2 ms earlier, and the battery sags with the current drawn. That's why the motor has to be computed at the physics rate.

Sensing shrinks by filtering, then taking one frame

All five truth frames pass through the firmware's pitch and yaw filters, because a filter's output depends on every sample it saw. The policy is shown only one frame per step, read from the observation ring with a delay.

Why latency is measured in 2 ms units

pipeline.tick pushes the held command into the delay ring on every tick, so each slot is 2 ms, not 10 ms. A drawn latency of three slots means 6 ms, even though the policy acts only every 10 ms.

Each mjlab hook calls the same wlbot/ object as the matching line in SingleRobot. The manager terms are adapters, not a second implementation.

Is the layer over-engineered?

Before judging, we checked what mjlab can do natively. "Just use mjlab's features" is only an alternative if the features exist.

What mjlab offers

  • BuiltinDcMotorActuator with a VOLTAGE mode and back-EMF, τ = K(V − Kω)/R, on MuJoCo's native <dcmotor>. Its docstring says that in this mode effort_target carries volts, so our apply_actions follows mjlab's convention. It's still dead code in our setup, because our compute ignores it.
  • Observation noise, delay and history in the observation manager, plus DR through events.

The real reason: four callers, one definition

A good test for over-engineering is the number of real callers, not the number of layers. The pipelines are plain batched torch with no mjlab imports, and they serve mjlab, PyBullet, Newton and the firmware-parity tests. Before the plant unification (finished 2026-09-14), mjlab had its own copies in actuators/mjlab_wheel_actuator.py and tasks/mdp/terms.py. The current working tree deletes both.

Our limitations: where it does feel forced

The friction is at the adapter boundary, where a 500 Hz plant is squeezed into a framework built around 100 Hz managers:

  • Sensing lives inside an actuator. WheelVoltageActuator.update() runs the sense pipeline because actuators are mjlab's only per-substep hook. An "actuator" that owns the IMU model surprises every new reader.
  • A hidden shared-state lookup. The action term, the observation term and the DR events all find the pipelines through wheel_actuator(entity). It's effectively a global, and the config doesn't show the dependency.
  • An ordering workaround. StationKeepObs keeps per-env step stamps because mjlab computes rewards before observations and resets envs in between.
  • torch.compile to recover speed. The generic per-substep ticks launch about 280 + 55 small kernels at 1024 envs, which native actuators would fuse.
  • Dead code. apply_actions writes an effort target that nothing reads.
  • Engine count. The bigger cost is the Newton adapter: newton_env.py is 929 lines, the largest file in the repo, for a judge-only engine. Agreement between simulators hasn't predicted the bench; improving the plant model has.

How other projects do it

No project gets "one loop we hand to the engine". A GPU physics engine never owns the control flow. Either you own the loop or a framework does, and this repo has one of each.

legged_gym · walk-these-ways · unitree_rl_gym

You write the loop

One env class. step() is literally a for decimation loop around _compute_torques and simulate. It's very readable.

The plant is thin: a PD law, with observation noise added once at the policy rate and no physics-rate filters. Deployment is a separate hand-written loop (deploy_mujoco.py) that copies the PD law and the observation building.

ANYmal's actuator network sits in the same _compute_torques slot. It's the closest analogue of our pipeline.tick.

Isaac Lab · mjlab

The framework owns the loop

Hooks are called at fixed moments. Isaac Lab offers two workflows: manager-based, and Direct, where one class implements _pre_physics_step (once) and _apply_action (decimation times).

mjlab ships only ManagerBasedRlEnv. We're a Direct-shaped env living in a manager-only framework, and that mismatch is most of the friction.

This is where we are.

MuJoCo Playground · Brax · MJX

The step is a pure function

No managers. Each env writes step(state, action), and substeps are a jax.lax.scan over mjx.step with ctrl held.

A custom scan body tick → mjx.step → sense would be SingleRobot.step compiled to GPU. It's the cleanest version of what we want.

The cost: rewrite the torch plant in JAX.

Two patterns hold across all three. The richer the plant, the more it strains a framework's hooks. legged_gym fits in one loop because its plant is a PD law, while ours has a battery, a 2 ms delay grid and firmware filters. And everyone who deploys has two loops, a vectorized training loop and a single-robot deployment loop. The only choice is whether the second one is a copy (unitree) or shares the plant code (us).

How we got to the decision

  • The layer is earned: four callers, framework-agnostic, and it replaced drifting copies.
  • Native mjlab would cover only part of the plant. A custom actuator would remain even if mjlab were the only engine.
  • The friction is real, but it costs readability, not correctness. Debating it further costs more than the friction does.
  • We already have the single loop: SingleRobot.step. The training side can get the same shape later through a Direct-style step() override, at the cost of owning a copy of mjlab's step (reset ordering, sim.forward(), the command manager, recorders).

Still open

  • Is each engine still paying for itself? What has Newton caught that PyBullet plus mjlab would not have?
  • Terminations at the policy rate. There are five sim.step() calls but one termination check per step. If pitch passes the fall limit at substep 2 and recovers by substep 5, what does the check see, and does it matter?

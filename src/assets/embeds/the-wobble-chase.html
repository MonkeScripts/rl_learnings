The Wobble Chase

A record of every run and test made while trying to reduce the balancing robot's wobble after sk_lean3. The short version: five policy experiments, one new physics engine and two analysis passes later, sk_lean3 model_4500 is still the best policy on the bench, and the simulator's wobble numbers turned out not to rank policies the way the robot does.

Why the mixer was restored (2026-09-18)

Four parallel investigations gave these reasons. The mixer's two outputs line up with the two tasks the observation already separates, balance and heading, so exploration noise on one does not disturb the other. A push recovery is a change in the balance channel alone, which costs half the slew penalty of the same recovery written as two wheel commands. The per-wheel policy had learned the mixer anyway (left and right commands correlated at 0.86) but its differential channel carried 56% of its energy above 5 Hz. The mixer in the code has no 8 V / 4 V split: both channels scale to 16 V and the wheel voltages clip at 8 V after mixing. Code: commit adf22f0.

The learning rate question

Before sk_lean2 the learning rate was fixed at 5e-4. Since then rsl_rl's adaptive schedule has been on with a KL target of 0.01. It adjusts after every mini-batch, which with 10 epochs and 32 mini-batches is 320 times per iteration: multiply by 1.5 when KL is under 0.005, divide by 1.5 when it is over 0.02, clamp between 1e-5 and 1e-2. The logged value is the last of the 320.

Actor step is the relative change in actor weights between checkpoints 100 iterations apart, over the last 40% of each run. The adaptive runs use far larger learning rates and still move the weights no further than the fixed runs, because the KL target caps each update.

The last two columns are the finding. A walk of n independent steps ends up 1/sqrt(n) of its path length from where it started, and every run matches that figure closely. After the plateau the weights wander with no direction under both schemes. A fixed rate never shrinks the step. The adaptive rule holds the policy change per update constant, so when the learning signal fades it raises the rate to keep moving, which is sk_lean3's climb to the ceiling. A longer run is a longer walk.

Practical consequence: checkpoints 100 to 500 iterations apart differ by 6 degrees of wobble and 0.3 of yaw-rate RMSE. Any comparison between runs that rests on one checkpoint from each is unreliable.

Averaging the late checkpoints

If late checkpoints are noise around a centre, the mean of their weights should sit nearer the centre than any of them. The average is the element-wise mean of every tensor in actor_state_dict. It works here because the files are consecutive snapshots of one converged run, so each hidden unit does the same job in all of them. Members sit 0.16 of the weight norm from a 21-checkpoint average and 0.07 to 0.10 from a 6-checkpoint one. Each average was evaluated from scratch.

The probe and the gates missed in both directions, so optimising the simulated wobble figure is not the same as optimising what the robot does. One untested explanation fits both misses. Policies that look calm in simulation make small corrections near upright, and on hardware small corrections can fall inside effects the simulation lacks at low amplitude: gear backlash, motor stiction, leg and servo compliance, delay jitter. A twitchier policy such as model_4500 would punch through them. The wobble probe's 4x friction rows are the nearest existing test and have not been run on the policies in question.

Newton with the Kamino solver as a third engine

Built to separate what belongs to the policy from what belongs to MuJoCo. wlbot_isaac/envs/newton_env.py wraps the solver directly and loads the mjlab MJCF with its loop closures. Isaac Lab's ManagerBasedRLEnv is not used, because its MJCF importer needs the full isaacsim runtime. The first crossplay read 0 of 1000 surviving, and every cause was in the wrapper.

The one real engine difference: the knee

With the hip held, Kamino's loop joint gives 1 to 2 mm under the roughly 12 N rod load at touchdown. wlbot/model/linkage.py puts the four-bar's two assembly branches 11.7 mm apart at 85 mm of leg height (17.4 mm at 75 mm) with about 1.8 mm of closure error between them, so at 83 mm and above the knee jumps toward the other branch and the leg folds. At 85 mm the linkage is still 0.096 rad of hip short of its singularity, so this is a branch jump and the toggle point is not involved. The env holds the knee at its kinematic angle with the hip servo's gains. The hold is idle on a healthy linkage and puts no net force on the robot. WLBOT_KNEE_HOLD=0 reproduces the fold. A sphere wheel collider was tried before the hold existed and wrongly credited with fixing a touchdown kick that was the knee. It was removed.

Open-loop parity against passive_reference.json passes with the MuJoCo test's tolerances. Speed on an RTX 5080: Kamino's solve is 21, 27 and 70 ms per 500 Hz substep at 256, 1024 and 4096 envs and takes 85 to 95% of the step. About 13 to 20 ms of that is launch overhead a CUDA graph would remove. The remaining 14 microseconds per env per substep is solver work, which caps this GPU near 14,000 env-steps per second against mjlab's 52,000. A 1000-episode gate takes 3 minutes. A 5000-iteration training run would take about 17 hours. Decision: train on mjlab and use Newton as a judge.

Wrong turns worth remembering

  • Two changes went into one commit (per-wheel and 128x128), and no later run separated them.
  • Runs were compared at different iteration counts and different push strengths. A 20,000-iteration run is at half push strength at 5000.
  • sk_lean4 was judged on its training reward and never gated for four days.
  • The sk_lean5_s0 kill alarm keyed on reward, which falls by construction when a weight switches on. Episode length was rising at the time.
  • A 4-episode wobble probe made model_9500 look like proof that longer training smooths the policy. At 12 episodes it was scatter.
  • "Per-wheel takes twice as long to learn heading" was concluded from sk_lean4 alone and reversed an hour later by sk_lean4b and 4c.
  • On Newton, sphere wheels were credited with a fix that belonged to the knee hold, because the two were never tested apart.
  • model_5500 was exported partly on the strength of a survival gate that the bench does not agree with.

Open questions

  • Whether model_4500's wobble matters for what the robot has to do, or whether it has been treated as a gate out of habit.
  • What differs on the bench between model_4500 and a policy that wobbles. A capture of the robot standing still under each (pitch, gyro, both action channels) would show the oscillation frequency and action amplitude, and whether the simulation can reproduce either. This is a plant-model question.
  • The 4x motor friction rows of the wobble probe on model_4500, model_5500 and the averaged policy.
  • Widening the training delay range to cover 40 to 45 ms. Untried, and any result would still be judged by a probe that has missed twice.
  • Annealing the step over the last third of a run (decay the KL target or the LR ceiling), so a run converges and checkpoints become comparable.
  • Per-wheel against 128x128, still unseparated. Low priority.
